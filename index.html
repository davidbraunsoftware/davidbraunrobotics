Highlights

All news
  • Apr 2025U.S. Actuator Patent GrantedThe patent covers the parallel variable stiffness actuator: a direct-drive motor combined with a spring of adjustable stiffness, arranged in parallel. The design combines precise force control with efficient energy storage for robots and wearable devices. “Method and Apparatus for Human Augmentation and Robot Actuation,” U.S. Patent 12,275,145 B2, by D. Braun and C. Mathews.
  • Mar 2022NSF CAREER AwardThis National Science Foundation CAREER Award supports research on mechanically adaptive robotics: robot actuators and wearable devices built around programmable springs. The stiffness of these springs can be changed to store and release energy as a task requires. The goal is a theoretical foundation for machines that enhance human physical abilities with little external energy.
  • Aug 2020NSF CMMI/DCSD GrantThis National Science Foundation grant supports research on real-time optimal control by experiment. Instead of relying on uncertain model predictions, the robot improves the motion during physical experiments. An approximate model only speeds up the computation, and measured data guide each improvement.
  • Jun 2014IEEE T-RO Best Paper AwardThe paper “Robots Driven by Compliant Actuators: Optimal Control under Actuation Constraints” received the 2013 King-Sun Fu Memorial IEEE Transactions on Robotics Best Paper Award, given each year to the best paper in the IEEE Transactions on Robotics. The award was presented at ICRA 2014 in Hong Kong. The paper shows how optimal control can exploit compliant actuators while safely respecting complex physical constraints: limits on actuator range, speed, and force. The method produced a human-like throwing motion without the use of human data.
